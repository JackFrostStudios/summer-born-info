# Summer-born Info — Writing Style Guide

This guide captures the tone of voice and copywriting patterns established for the Summer-born Info website. Use it as a reference when writing new pages, cards, buttons, or microcopy, so the site feels consistent as it grows.

---

## 1. Core Principles

**1. Speak to the parent, not about the policy.**
Put the parent's decision and their child at the centre of every sentence. Avoid framing content around what a system or authority "allows" — frame it around what the parent can do.

> ❌ "Allowing summer-born children to start school when they are ready."
> ✅ "Help your summer-born child start school at the right time for them."

**2. Defuse worry before it's spoken.**
Parents researching this topic often carry an unspoken fear (e.g. "will my child be behind?" or "will a council say no?"). Where relevant, name the worry plainly and reassure directly, rather than avoiding it.

> ✅ "It's not about holding them back — it's about giving them the best possible start."

**3. Be honest about what a page or button actually does.**
Never imply a feature, continuity, or outcome that isn't real. Copy should be a promise the site can actually keep.

> ❌ "We'll help you continue from where you were." *(if no progress is actually saved)*
> ✅ "Back to where you were." *(accurate — the button does return them to their last page)*

**4. Plain English over jargon or internal terminology.**
Avoid acronyms, build/dev labels, or internal categorisation that hasn't been written for a parent audience (e.g. avoid raw labels like "SBI" or "Now / Later" without context).

**5. No unverifiable claims or sales language.**
Avoid marketing phrases like "trusted by thousands of parents" or dated claims that can't be substantiated. Trust is built through clarity and honesty, not social proof or urgency.

---

## 2. Tone

- **Warm but credible** — supportive like a knowledgeable friend, not clinical like a government form, and not salesy like a marketing site.
- **Calm, not urgent** — this is a considered decision, not an impulse action. Avoid pressure language ("Don't miss out," "Act now").
- **Confident, not hedging** — state things plainly. Avoid excessive qualifiers ("might possibly," "in some cases perhaps") where the guidance is clear.
- **Concise** — say the necessary thing in as few words as possible. Cut extra sentences that repeat an idea already made.

---

## 3. Structural Patterns

### Headings
Keep headings literal and specific rather than abstract or stylised. A parent should understand what a section is about from the heading alone.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Legal Rights | Know Your Power |
| How to Apply | Your Roadmap |
| Common Questions | FAQ *(fine if space-constrained, but "Common Questions" reads warmer)* |

### Body copy (cards / sections)
Use a consistent two-sentence shape where possible:
1. **Name the parent's real question or worry.**
2. **State plainly what the site does about it.**

> **Example — Legal Rights**
> "Delaying your child's start isn't a favour a council can choose to grant — it's a right set out in law. We break down what the guidance actually says, in plain English, so you know where you stand before you make your case."

Keep card body copy to 2–3 sentences maximum. Longer explanations belong on the linked detail page, not the summary card.

### Calls to action (buttons)
Use the pattern: **[Verb] the/your [specific thing] →**

- Specific, not vague — name what the parent will get, not just "click here" or "learn more."
- Consistent structure across all buttons on a page or section, so the grid feels intentional.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Read the full guide → | Learn more |
| Understand your rights → | Click here |
| Browse the FAQs → | Find out more |
| See the step-by-step guide → | Start your journey *(too generic/overused)* |
| Back to where you were → | Click here to go back |

## 5. Quick Checklist Before Publishing Copy

- [ ] Is the parent (not the policy or the system) the subject of the sentence?
- [ ] Does this copy name a real worry, if one is relevant, rather than skip past it?
- [ ] Is every claim something the page/button can actually deliver?
- [ ] Have I removed jargon, acronyms, and internal labels?
- [ ] Is the CTA specific and in the "[Verb] the [thing] →" pattern?
- [ ] Could this sentence be shorter without losing meaning?
- [ ] Does the tone match "knowledgeable, warm friend" rather than "government form" or "sales page"?
