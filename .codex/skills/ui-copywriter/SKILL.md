# UI Copywriter

Use this skill whenever Summer-born Info UI work needs new or changed parent-facing copy.

## Source Of Truth

- Read [writing-style-guide.md](writing-style-guide.md) before drafting or reviewing UI copy.
- Treat that guide as the canonical voice, tone, and CTA reference for the site.
- If code or a plan conflicts with the style guide, preserve behaviour and product truth first, then adapt the wording to the guide.

## Copy Goals

- Speak directly to parents and keep their child, choice, and next step at the centre of the sentence.
- Defuse likely worries plainly when the surrounding UI raises them.
- Make every heading, button, and sentence an accurate promise about what the page or action really does.
- Use plain English instead of policy jargon, acronyms, internal labels, or sales language.
- Keep the tone warm, calm, credible, confident, and concise.

## UI Patterns

- Prefer literal, specific headings such as "Legal Rights", "How to Apply", and "Common Questions".
- For cards and section summaries, use the guide's two-sentence shape where it fits: name the parent's real question or worry, then state what the site does about it.
- Keep card body copy to 2-3 sentences; move deeper explanation to the destination page.
- Write CTAs as specific actions in the pattern "[Verb] the/your [specific thing] ->" when space allows.
- Avoid vague CTAs such as "Learn more", "Click here", "Find out more", or "Start your journey" unless the product context leaves no clearer option.

## Review Checklist

Before handing off copy, check that:

- The parent, not the policy or system, is the focus.
- Relevant worries are named and answered calmly.
- Claims are true for the implemented page or action.
- Jargon, acronyms, internal labels, urgency, and unverifiable claims are removed.
- CTAs are specific and consistent across neighbouring buttons.
- Sentences are as short as they can be without losing meaning.
